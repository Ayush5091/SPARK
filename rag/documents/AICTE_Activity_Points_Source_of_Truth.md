## 1. Source hierarchy and authority

Use the following priority when answering questions:

1.  **Current VTU regulations applicable to the student's batch/scheme**
    --- highest priority for VTU students.
2.  **Current VTU notifications/circulars** implementing Activity Points
    for a particular academic year or regulation.
3.  **Official AICTE Internship Policy / AICTE Activity Point Programme
    material** --- national baseline and source for the Activity Point
    Programme.
4.  **Older VTU regulations/annexures** --- use only when the student's
    scheme/batch is covered by them.
5.  Never treat third-party summaries, college websites, social-media
    posts, or student-generated explanations as authoritative when an
    official AICTE/VTU source is available.

### Important interpretation rule

**AICTE Activity Points are not one universal rule whose operational
details are identical at every university.** AICTE provides the
programme framework, while universities such as VTU incorporate it into
their own degree regulations and notifications.

Therefore, if a student asks a question such as **"How many points do I
need?"**, the assistant should first determine:

-   University / affiliating authority
-   Degree type
-   Regulation / scheme
-   Admission year or batch
-   Entry route: regular, lateral entry, or transfer
-   Whether the question concerns a national AICTE rule or a
    university-specific implementation

If the university/batch is unknown, do not confidently apply a
VTU-specific rule.

# 2. Core concept: What are AICTE Activity Points?

The AICTE Activity Point Programme is a **non-credit programme**
intended to encourage activities beyond conventional academic
coursework.

The official AICTE policy describes the purpose in terms of developing:

-   Soft skills
-   Leadership qualities
-   Team spirit
-   Entrepreneurial capabilities
-   Societal commitment

Activity Points are awarded **over and above academic grades**.

They are associated with community service and allied activities and are
treated separately from normal academic credits.

# 3. AICTE national baseline

## 3.1 Minimum points in the AICTE Internship Policy

The official AICTE Internship Policy states:

  Student entry category                           Minimum Activity Points
  ---------------------------------------------- -------------------------
  4-year degree programme, regular entry                           **100**
  4-year degree programme, lateral entry                            **75**
  3-year diploma programme, regular entry                           **75**
  Diploma programme, lateral entry in 2nd year                      **50**

The AICTE policy states that these points are an additional requirement
alongside academic requirements.

### Source

Official AICTE Internship Policy, Chapter 6 --- AICTE Activity Point
Programme.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3.2 AICTE recommended activity hours

The official AICTE Internship Policy states:

-   Degree students: AICTE recommends **300--400 hours** for community
    service and allied activities.
-   Diploma students: AICTE recommends **200--250 hours** for community
    service and allied activities.
-   **40--45 hours are treated as approximately one week** in the
    policy's activity-duration framework.

### Important distinction

Do **not** automatically interpret "100 Activity Points" as "100 hours".

Points and hours are different concepts. The programme uses specified
activities, minimum durations/hours, evidence, and evaluation to
determine points.

# 4. AICTE Activity Point Programme --- official activity framework

The official AICTE Activity Point Programme includes activity areas such
as:

1.  Preparing and implementing a plan to create local job opportunities.
2.  Preparing and implementing a plan to improve education quality in a
    village.
3.  Preparing an actionable DPR for doubling village income.
4.  Developing a sustainable water management system.
5.  Preparing/improving a plan to improve health parameters of
    villagers.
6.  Developing and implementing low-cost sanitation facilities.
7.  Preparing and implementing a plan to promote local tourism through
    innovative approaches.
8.  Implementing/developing technology solutions that improve quality of
    life.
9.  Preparing and implementing solutions for energy conservation.
10. Preparing and implementing a plan to skill village youth and provide
    employment.
11. Developing localized techniques for reduction in construction cost.
12. Preparing and implementing a plan for sustainable growth of a
    village.
13. Setting up an information-imparting club for women contributing to
    social and economic issues.
14. Developing and managing an efficient garbage disposal system.
15. Contributing to a national-level Government of India initiative, for
    example Digital India, Skill India, or Swachh Bharat Internship.

The official AICTE activity summary describes these as **suggestive
activities**; the programme allows students to choose activities
according to their interests, subject to the applicable institutional
rules and evidence/evaluation requirements.

# 5. Activity duration and points in the AICTE activity summary

The official AICTE activity summary gives the following general
structure for the listed degree activities:

-   **Degree:** 2 weeks / approximately **80--90 hours** for an activity
    carrying a maximum of **20 points**.
-   **Diploma:** 1 week / approximately **40--45 hours** for the
    corresponding activity framework.
-   Activities are non-credit.
-   Activities may be undertaken during weekends/holidays and spread
    across the programme period, subject to the applicable rules.
-   Evidence/documentation and evaluation are part of the process.

### Critical RAG rule

Do not say that **every arbitrary activity automatically gives 20
points**.

The 20-point value applies to the specified activity framework and its
required duration/evaluation. A student's actual awarded points depend
on the applicable university/institutional rules and appraisal.

# 7. Current VTU 2025 hour framework

The VTU 2025 regulations specify:

        Points   Approximate activity time
  ------------ ---------------------------
     20 points            **80--90 hours**
     50 points          **200--225 hours**
     75 points          **300--338 hours**
    100 points          **400--450 hours**

The regulation also states that:

-   The time required for 20 points **does not have to come from one
    single activity**.
-   Hours may be accumulated across multiple activities.
-   The duration counted includes time from **preparation through
    completion** of the activity.
-   Students may undertake activities during free time on working days,
    weekends, holidays, and vacations.

### Important version distinction

The older AICTE policy gives a national recommendation of **300--400
hours for degree students' community service and allied activities**,
while the current VTU 2025 regulations specify **400--450 hours for 100
VTU Activity Points**.

These numbers should **not be merged into one rule**.

For a VTU student covered by the 2025 regulations, use the **current VTU
requirement** when answering questions about their degree requirement.

# 8. Current VTU completion deadline and degree impact

Under the VTU 2025 regulations:

-   All prescribed Activity Points must be completed **before
    commencement of the 8th-semester examinations**.
-   If the student has not earned the prescribed points before the
    8th-semester examinations, the **8th-semester Grade Card is withheld
    until the required points are earned**.
-   The student becomes eligible for award of the degree only after the
    8th-semester Grade Card is released.
-   Activity Points are reflected on the **8th-semester/final Grade
    Card**.
-   Activity Points are **non-credit**.
-   Activity Points do **not affect SGPA or CGPA**.

### Do not confuse these concepts

-   **Non-credit** does not mean optional.
-   Under VTU's applicable regulations, Activity Points can be
    **mandatory for degree award** even though they do not contribute
    academic credits or CGPA.

# 9. Current VTU activity selection

The VTU 2025 regulations allow students to:

-   Select activities from the prescribed activity list.
-   Select another relevant contemporary topic that is advantageous to
    society, **in consultation with the AICTE Activity Points Programme
    Coordinator**.
-   Accumulate the required time across activities rather than
    necessarily completing one single activity.

Therefore, the assistant should not tell a student:

> "Only the activities in this list are allowed."

A safer and more accurate answer is:

> "The prescribed activity list provides approved/suggestive areas. VTU
> also allows another relevant contemporary topic advantageous to
> society when selected in consultation with the AICTE Activity Points
> Programme Coordinator."

# 10. Current VTU coordination, evidence and certification

The VTU 2025 regulations state that:

-   Activities are coordinated by the appropriate **NSS / NCC / Sports /
    Training and Placement Officer**, as applicable.
-   Every student must maintain a file containing details of activities
    undertaken and appropriate **documentary evidence**.
-   The activity file is verified and certified by the concerned
    evaluator.
-   The Principal / Chairperson / Programme Coordinator submits the list
    of students who completed the Activity Points to the University,
    along with the relevant 8th-semester CIE information.
-   The college issues an **AICTE Activity Points completion
    certificate**, endorsed by the concerned Activity Points
    Coordinator.

### Practical implication for the RAG assistant

If a student asks:

**"Do I need proof?"**

Answer **yes**, for VTU's current framework: students are required to
maintain documentary evidence and have the activity file
verified/certified.

Do not invent a specific document type unless the applicable activity
table/institution specifies it.

# 12. Long-term rural/community goals

The AICTE Internship Policy also discusses long-term goals under the
Rural Internship Programme.

Examples include:

-   Creating local job opportunities.
-   Improving education quality in a village.
-   Preparing an actionable DPR for doubling village income.
-   Sustainable water management.
-   Improving health parameters of villagers.
-   Low-cost sanitation.
-   Promoting local tourism.
-   Technology solutions that improve quality of life.
-   Energy conservation.

The policy distinguishes between:

-   **Long-term goals** completed during the degree programme, which may
    be counted as Internship Activity with the internship requirement
    fulfilled; and
-   **Short-term interventions**, which may count toward the AICTE
    Activity Point Programme requirement.

Do not automatically classify every rural project as both an internship
and Activity Points activity. The applicable programme and institutional
approval matter.

# 14. Important discrepancy: AICTE vs VTU wording

The RAG assistant must preserve the distinction between these sources.

### AICTE policy baseline

AICTE's official Internship Policy describes:

-   100 points for regular 4-year degree entry.
-   75 points for lateral entry.
-   75 points for regular diploma entry.
-   50 points for diploma lateral entry.
-   300--400 recommended hours for degree community service/allied
    activities.
-   200--250 recommended hours for diploma community service/allied
    activities.

### Current VTU 2025 B.E./B.Tech regulation

VTU specifies:

-   100 points for regular 4-year B.E./B.Tech entry.
-   75 points for lateral entry / university transfer to 3rd semester.
-   50 points for transfer to 5th semester.
-   400--450 hours for 100 points.
-   300--338 hours for 75 points.

### Correct response strategy

If the student is under VTU's current 2025 B.E./B.Tech regulations,
**use the VTU numbers for the student's degree requirement**.

If the student asks specifically:

then report the AICTE policy figures.

Never combine the two figures into an invented universal formula.

# 17. RAG answer rules

The assistant should follow these rules strictly.

## Rule 1 --- Never invent point values

Do not say an activity gives 10, 15, 20, 30, etc. points unless the
retrieved authoritative source explicitly supports that value.

## Rule 2 --- Never invent eligibility

Do not claim that a certificate, hackathon, blood donation, internship,
NSS activity, workshop, webinar, online course, competition, or project
automatically earns Activity Points unless the applicable source
supports it.

## Rule 3 --- Separate "can qualify" from "automatically qualifies"

Preferred wording:

> "This may qualify if it is accepted under the applicable Activity
> Point framework and approved/evaluated by the college coordinator."

Avoid:

> "Yes, you will definitely get 20 points."

## Rule 4 --- Do not confuse hours with points

Hours are an activity-duration requirement. Points are the award
mechanism.

## Rule 5 --- Batch matters

VTU regulations and notifications can change. If a student gives a
batch/year, use the source applicable to that batch.

## Rule 6 --- Prefer current official sources

For VTU students, prefer the latest applicable VTU
regulation/notification over older annexures.

## Rule 7 --- Do not use third-party sites as the final authority

Search engines, student forums, blogs, Telegram posts, and college
summaries can be useful for discovery but should not override an
official AICTE/VTU source.

## Rule 8 --- Be explicit about uncertainty

If no authoritative source was found for a specific activity:

> "I could not verify that activity as an automatic Activity Point
> category from the official AICTE/VTU sources available to me. Please
> confirm with your college's Activity Points Coordinator."

## Rule 9 --- Do not fabricate current policy

If the model does not have a current official source for a rule, it
should say so rather than assuming that an older policy is still
unchanged.
